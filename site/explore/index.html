Seafolk · the map

Where the boats are

Every fleet on one chart, month by month. Drag the month, switch a fleet off, zoom into a harbour.

…

    No dot is a boat. Each patch of sea gets dots in proportion to how much its fleet was out there, placed at random inside the patch.

    The chart

    Every ship at sea carries a radio that says, every few seconds, where it is. Denmark publishes all of it, small boats included, and I have been counting what it heard. This is the count on a chart, one colour per fleet in the inks a chart gives them: magenta for cargo, red for ferries, green for fishing, orange for the small boats.

    The dots

    Working ships are counted in ship-hours: one ship, moving through a patch the size of a town, for one hour. Small boats are counted in boat-days, over patches the size of a small island, coarse on purpose so that no single boat can be picked out of one; every patch drawn holds at least five different boats, and below that the archive keeps its mouth shut and so do I.

    The months

    The slider only offers the months I actually loaded: the years 2015, 2018 and 2021, everything from spring 2024 to today, and a handful of single months around big storms. The years in between are missing because I never loaded them, not because the sea was empty.

    Source: the Danish Maritime Authority's AIS archive (aisdata.ais.dk), counted by scripts/build_explore_data.py from the open dataset in this repository; depths from EMODnet Bathymetry, drawn by scripts/charts_explore.py.
